    NASA SYSTEMS ENGINEERING & MISSION DESIGN GUIDE

    Fundamental principles of aerospace trade studies and spacecraft subsystems

    1. The Core Systems Engineering Challenge

    Every space mission is an intricate balance of competing physical constraints. No single subsystem can be optimized in isolation without penalizing another:

    • More Instruments: Increases science discovery, but drives up mass, power demands, radio data rates, and total cost.
    • Higher Orbit (e.g. GEO or L2): Eliminates atmospheric drag and expands field of view, but drastically reduces the payload mass a rocket can deliver.
    • Larger Solar Arrays: Generate more power, but introduce structural mass penalties and attitude-pointing drag.

    2. Key Spacecraft Subsystems

    🛰️ Spacecraft Bus

    The structural skeleton that protects avionics, supports mounting brackets, and bears launch acoustic loads.

    ⚡ Power Subsystem (EPS)

    Triple-junction solar cells or RTG nuclear power. Note that solar irradiance follows the inverse-square law (\(1/r^2\)) at Mars.

    🎯 Attitude Determination & Control (ADCS)

    Reaction wheels and star trackers that point cameras at targets with arcsecond precision and maintain antenna alignment.

    📡 Telecommunications

    RF carrier links (UHF, S, X, Ka-band) and directional high-gain antennas to downlink data to ground stations.

    3. Flight Readiness & Evaluation Rubric

    Before liftoff, missions undergo a rigorous Flight Readiness Review (FRR). Missions are scored across four key pillars:

    • Science Return (40%): Fulfillment of specific mission objectives, sensor diversity, and observation quality.
    • Systems Engineering (25%): Health of mass and power margins, pointing stability alignment, and launcher reliability.
    • Fiscal Efficiency (20%): Completing objectives within designated budget envelopes without over-spending.
    • Operational Longevity (15%): Maintaining spacecraft health through solar flares, debris risks, and component aging.

    NASA Mission Control & 3D Flight Operations Guide

    🚀

    1. Rocket Liftoff & Ascent

    • [A] / [D] or [←] / [→] : Steer rocket into the green flight corridor.
    • [SPACEBAR] : Skip countdown, trigger MECO Stage 1 separation, and jettison payload fairing.
    • Maintain corridor alignment to minimize aerodynamic Max-Q drag and reach vacuum orbit!
    🛰️

    2. Orbit Operations & Science

    • [W] : Prograde Burn to raise your orbit altitude.
    • [S] : Retrograde Burn to lower periapsis for de-orbit.
    • [SPACEBAR] : Scan Earth phenomena (cyclones, polar ice, volcanoes) for Science XP points!
    • Watch solar eclipse umbra: heaters turn on to protect avionics from cold soak!
